- Rhetorical closing questions must point at genuine open questions or documented contradictions in the case—not fake cliffhangers or made-up twists.

LENGTH (Shorts feed — match punchy, rewatch-friendly runtime):
- Target ~28–50 seconds when read aloud. The pipeline HARD-CAPS at 95 words — if you write more, extra lines are discarded, so stay UNDER the cap.
- ~60–90 words (STRICT). Never exceed 90 words. Count words before you finish; one word over the cap breaks the pacing goal.

STYLE:
- Fast-paced
- Simple English

- Tone: Chilling, mysterious, and fast-paced. US YouTube mystery / true-crime Shorts — urgent, plainspoken narrator; not a slow documentary lecture

STRUCTURE (follow this 4-part order — do not skip any section):

1. HOOK (first 1–2 sentences — CRITICAL)
- Open with the strangest documented 'sound', 'last known evidence', or sensory detail from the case.
- Must be concrete and searchable: a recorded transmission, a physical anomaly, an impossible detail.
- No throat-clearing, no "one of history's greatest mysteries" padding. Drop viewers into the strangeness immediately.

2. THE FACT (next 2–3 sentences)
- State the core case overview: YEAR, full NAME, PLACE — plainly and precisely.
- Only verified facts. Each sentence adds exactly one new fact or documented beat.
- No filler, no backstory paragraphs.

3. THE DEEP DIVE (1–2 sentences)
- One detail most viewers won't know: a documented anomaly, a contradiction in the official record, or a specific piece of overlooked evidence.
- You may include ONE widely-discussed popular theory (e.g. from forums, documentaries) — but label it clearly as unverified: use phrases like "Some investigators believe…", "One theory suggests…", or "Nobody has explained why…". NEVER present theory as fact.

4. THE CLIFFHANGER (final 1–2 sentences — loop-friendly)
- End with a direct question or open paradox aimed at the viewer.
- Must point at a REAL unresolved aspect or documented contradiction — never a fake twist.
- Tone: leave the viewer unsettled, not satisfied.

BOOKEND (mandatory — opening and closing must connect):
- Pick ONE concrete anchor from your HOOK (the sound, object, place, transmission, or last-known evidence you opened with).
- The CLIFFHANGER must explicitly call back to that same anchor: reuse its noun, restate the paradox around it, or ask a question that only lands because of the opening beat.
- Do NOT end with a generic closer ("Will we ever know?", "Some secrets stay buried") unless it clearly names or unmistakably refers back to that opening anchor.
- Do NOT introduce a new focal mystery, object, or person in the final sentences that was absent from the HOOK and FACT beats—stay on one spine.
- The viewer should feel the ending was inevitable from how the Short began.

## When Wikipedia + fact bullets are in the user message (this pipeline)
- The script must not be "vague mood + a single question." Lead with 1–2 **searchable** specifics that the sources allow (a year, a full name, a place, or one documented legal outcome). Mystery lands harder when a few **hard** facts are clear first.
- The fact bullets are the spine: weave their KEY ANCHORS (if provided) into the first half of the script in plain, declarative English—then tension and questions.
- If the sources are thin, still name the people or case from the topic; use short cold sentences; the ending should point at a **documented** open question, not empty hype.
